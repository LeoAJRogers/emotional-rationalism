# Open problems and related research

- **Measurement.** Self-report, expression, and physiology can diverge. Test whether the proposed affect coordinates are stable across repeated trials and comparable across participants.
- **Identifiability.** Several combinations of belief update, affect persistence, and measurement noise can produce the same observed lag. Use repeated measurements and independently varied cues and prompts to test which parameters can be recovered.
- **Fittingness.** Different evidential standards or values can yield different minimizers. Document disagreement and test sensitivity to the choice of loss before treating the optimum as a target.
- **Feasible actions.** Regulatory choices are partly unobserved and context dependent. A restricted action set can underestimate attainable responses. A model that grants participants unavailable information can overestimate them.
- **Competing explanations.** Delayed ratings may follow measurement artifacts, demand effects, baseline affect persistence, or changing context. Look for predictions that distinguish the proposed model from these alternatives and from existing appraisal and regulation accounts.

## Integration questions (2026-11-19)

- **Separate dimensions.** Test affect, social-feature, belief, parameter, and measurement complexity independently. Rotations and reparameterizations of latent representations can defeat claims of unique dimensions.
- **Decomposition.** Determine whether observation/inference G and affect transition T can be separately identified, especially when uncertainty is itself affectively salient and emotions affect attention.
- **Perspective/roles.** Full information can coexist with disagreement. Define which role/relationship-preserving observer substitutions ought to yield equivalent predictions and which should not.
- **Normative reference sensitivity.** An evidential reference distribution and declared values define a conditional evaluative exercise, not an objective single best feeling; compare reasonable alternative references and criteria.
- **Trajectory selection.** Selecting hypothetical distributions for analysis is distinct from inducing affect. Joint constraints on revised appraisals can conflict; document consistency, reference dependence, and update-order effects.
- **Information regimes.** Honest missing information, inaccurate inference, concealment, and deception are different interventions. Test whether each needs additional model structure or can be handled by a shared transition kernel.
- **Fallible evaluators.** Distinguishing descriptive emotion from evidence-based evaluation does not require participants to evaluate accurately, or to evaluate at all. The researcher-side reference q and criteria are themselves fallible; test the consequences of inaccurate assessments without conflating these mistakes with absence of social dynamics.
- **Felt versus reported.** A change in explicit judgment, urgency, or choice does not alone demonstrate change in felt emotional response. Multiple temporal and behavioral measures remain imperfect.

## Starting literature

- Klaus R. Scherer (2009), *The dynamic architecture of emotion: Evidence for the component process model*. A comparator for sequential appraisal and coordinated emotion components. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930902928969)
- James J. Gross (1998), *Antecedent- and response-focused emotion regulation: Divergent consequences for experience, expression, and physiology*. An experiment comparing reappraisal and suppression across response measures. [Paper record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9457784/)
- Anil K. Seth (2013), *Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self*. A theoretical comparator for the role of inference about bodily signals. [Paper record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24126130/)
