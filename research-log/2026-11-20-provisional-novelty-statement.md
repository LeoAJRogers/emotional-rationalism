# Proposed novelty and priority statement

**Recorded:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** Provisional research contribution; priority documented, novelty not established by exhaustive prior-art review.

## Narrow proposed contribution

Emotional Rationalism proposes a testable separation of (1) stochastic affective-state transitions, (2) conditional or set-valued evaluations of which responses fit specified evidence and values, and (3) the probability of reaching candidate states under specified actions, information, and time horizons.

The proposed research contribution is **not** that appraisal, emotion dynamics, regulation, or reachability are individually new. It is the explicit joint formalization, operational separation, and comparative testing of these components, with particular attention to history dependence, observation noise, heterogeneity, and identifiability.

## What current evidence does and does not support

As documented in the 2026-11-20 public integration checkpoint, experience-sampling analyses found modest out-of-sample predictive gains from earlier affect reports, with substantial sensitivity to period-level differences. Synthetic controls show that apparent history and individual effects can arise from filtering and reporting-scale artifacts. A randomized MBCT contrast concerns reported affect, not a confirmed latent transition mechanism. The same-source numerical cross-check is **not** independent-sample replication.

These analyses **do not yet test** the framework's distinctive fittingness/reachability separation, belief-to-affect lag, or social coupling. Accordingly, they cannot currently establish that the proposed joint formulation predicts better than strong existing alternatives.

## Novelty hypotheses to investigate

1. **Formal separation:** Does the specific three-part formulation differ materially from existing computational appraisal, affective dynamics, emotion regulation, control/POMDP, and reachability frameworks?
2. **Discriminating predictions:** Does keeping descriptive transition, evaluative fittingness, and constrained reachability separate yield falsifiable predictions or interventions that an otherwise comparable baseline cannot capture?
3. **Empirical value:** On held-out participants and independent datasets, does the added structure improve calibrated predictions, explain identifiable variation, or support better counterfactual tests after accounting for measurement error and person/period heterogeneity?
4. **Minimality:** Can simpler models reproduce the results? If so, narrow or abandon the claim of distinctive mechanism.

## Claims deliberately not made

- That Emotional Rationalism is the first theory of emotional state transitions.
- That any of its component ideas are individually unprecedented.
- That reported affect directly identifies a latent emotional state.
- That a preferred or evaluated response is universally correct, consciously selectable, or attainable.
- That current data establish a new psychological law, causal mechanism, cross-species generalization, or clinical benefit.
