## Bottom line

1. **The pooled evidence that motivated R-006 does not favour item-level dynamics.**
   - R-005 found NA "within-item excess" in pooled real data. In synthetic worlds with **no** item-specific persistence, floors plus person-specific means plus loading heterogeneity produce pooled excess of **+0.27 to +0.69**. It fires in 100% of datasets.
   - Mechanism: with floors, each person's mean sets their observed-metric loading, and pooling turns loading differences into apparent item persistence. The exact pooled equivalence is E1 below.
   - Caveat: R-005's statistic was not available to me, so the comparison is by mechanism, not number.
2. **Within-person restrictions remove that confound.**
   - Under person-specific loadings, within-person item excess fires in **3%** of datasets; the pooled version fires in **99%**.
   - It stays at 1–4% under heterogeneous inertia, momentary method factors, missingness that depends on NA, and realistic floors.
   - It detects item-specific persistence of 0.25 in **98%** of realistic datasets. Power drops to **29%** at 0.10.
3. **What 0010 can and cannot identify.**
   - **Identifiable:** one shared persistent NA dimension versus extra persistence beyond it. And, through a sign theorem, a second shared persistent dimension versus item-specific persistence (57% and 30% power in realistic worlds).
   - **Not identifiable from these data:**
     - persistent affect versus a persistent response style with the same keying;
     - PA/NA versus valence + acquiescence;
     - item persistence versus response anchoring;
     - two timescales within NA (25% power);
     - and, except at low power, item persistence versus loading drift between days.
4. **9 of 11 preregistered predictions held**, including the decisive one (A2) and the go/no-go gate (A8).
   - **A5 failed:** the floor artefact in the within-person statistic is much smaller than I predicted.
   - **A6 failed in part:** the rate-difference test T2 is nearly powerless here, so the sign test carries that question.
5. **Part B is preregistered with guards calibrated to the real data's own missingness and item distributions.** It runs as soon as the file is available.

## 2. Two premise challenges

**(a) A held-out predictive race is the wrong primary tool for this question.**

- Under person-level heterogeneity, a population-level F1+R or F2 really does predict better than a population-level F1. It is a better approximation to a mixture. #45's false verdicts are "pseudo-true" for that reason, and they grow with more data.
- Scoring held-out *people* also requires integrating person parameters over an assumed population distribution. The verdict then depends on that assumption.
- The question "which structure is identified" is better asked of the restrictions each structure implies *within* a person:
  - **F1 makes every lag-k (k ≥ 1) cross-covariance matrix rank one**, with that person's own loadings. Two vanishing tetrads follow: the item excess T1 and the pair-rate minors T2.
  - That is Bollen & Ting's confirmatory tetrad analysis (1993; bootstrap version in [Bollen & Ting, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124198027001002)), applied to within-person lagged covariances. [Hipp & Bollen (2003, *Sociological Methodology* 33, 267–305)](https://www.cpc.unc.edu/resources/publications/bib/2086/) extended vanishing-tetrad tests to ordinal variables.
  - The new part is narrow: computing the tetrads *within person* with disjoint-pair U-statistics. No noise term then appears twice, so the restrictions hold exactly under F1 for any shared-factor process and any missingness that depends on the shared factor. The proof sketch is in DESIGN.md §5.

**(b) "Dimensionality of emotional dynamics" has to be measured from lagged moments.**

- Same-beep factor structure counts every common source: momentary affect, momentary response styles, careless answering, wording overlap.
- Covariances between *different* beeps keep only what persists. This is the idea behind [Lam & Yao (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1214/12-AOS970): white-noise components drop out of lag ≥ 1 autocovariances.
- So the immunity to momentary artefacts comes from where the measurement is taken, not from modelling them. Part A confirms it: a momentary method factor moves the static/dynamic contrast T2₀ in 100% of datasets, and T1 and T2 in 1.5% and 6.5%.

## 3. Identification map (exact; `equivalences.py`)

| | Equivalent pair (cannot be told apart) | Consequence for R-006 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E1** | **Pooled:** F1 with person-specific loadings ≡ F1 + item residuals decaying at the factor's rate | Pooled item excess cannot separate individual differences from item dynamics. This explains #45's 96% and its "ρ ≈ φ" warning. With floors, person-specific *means* create person-specific observed loadings, so E1 applies even with identical latent loadings. |
| **E2** | **Pooled:** heterogeneous inertia ≡ one dimension with two timescales | Pooled lag profiles cannot show a second timescale ([Granger, 1980](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(80)90092-5)). |
| **E3a** (new) | **Not an equivalence; a theorem.** For 3 items under any pure two-factor persistence, `g_W·g_F·g_S = −(xyz)² ≤ 0` | A second shared persistent dimension always leaves some item with non-positive excess, which item-specific persistence never does. That gives an identified sign test. |
| **E3b** | F2 + item residuals ≡ F1 + item residuals (equal rates) | Item persistence cannot exclude "second factor plus item persistence". |
| **E4** | Separate PA and NA persistence ≡ bipolar valence + persistent acquiescence (equal rates; exact rotation) | Two persistent dimensions are identifiable; *which* two is not. This is the dynamic face of the bipolarity debate ([Russell & Carroll, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.1.3); [Green, Goldman & Salovey, 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.6.1029); "dynamic bipolarity" in [Green, Salovey & Truax, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.76.5.856)). |
| **E5** | A persistent reporting process on NA-keyed items ≡ persistent NA | 0010 has no reverse-keyed NA items and no anchors. Not testable here. |

The floor result connects to an old psychometric finding: nonlinearity produces spurious "difficulty" factors when items differ in difficulty ([McDonald & Ahlawat, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8317.1974.tb00530.x)). Here the same thing happens dynamically. Floored items acquire extra Hermite components that decay as φⁿ and look like item-specific or second-factor persistence.

## 4. Part A: what the bench showed

22 worlds matched to 0010's design, 200 replicates each. Rates are the share of datasets in which a statistic fires; full tables are in `results/`.

| Question | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Is within-person T1 robust to heterogeneity? | **Yes.** Person-specific loadings: 3% (pooled 99%). Person-specific inertia: 4%. MNAR: 2%. Momentary method factor: 1.5%. Realistic ordinal null N8: 1.5%; with stronger heterogeneity (N9): 4%. |
| How large is pooled T1 with no item persistence? | Loadings log-SD 0.3: +0.09. Floors 0.45/0.65/0.50: +0.27. Heavy floors: +0.69. Realistic N8: +0.39. |
| Power for item persistence | Gaussian: 100% at 0.25, 99.5% at 0.10. Realistic (corrected): 98% at 0.25, 29% at 0.10. |
| Second shared dimension | T2: 27.5% (Gaussian, slow factor), 1% realistic. **Sign test:** 100% and 97.5% Gaussian; realistic 74% / 51% (95% intervals), 57% / 30% (Bonferroni, fresh seeds). |
| Declared confounds | Between-day loading drift: T1 fires in **95%** (same-day variant 6%). Anchoring: 100%. Neither is separable by T1. |
| Two timescales within NA | T3: 25.5%. Untestable here. |
| Bipolar persistence | Separate PA/NA persistence: 100%; one bipolar dimension: 2%. |

**What surprised me:**

- **The within-person statistic is precise.** SD 0.023 in Gaussian worlds, against my guess of 0.063.
- **Floors barely bias it.** Linear bias is at most +0.03, against my prediction of ≥ +0.03 with > 10% false positives.
- **The ordinal correction is conservative and unstable under heavy floors.**
- **Exploratory, post-Part-A:** turning off the day jackknife biases the corrected statistic by −0.12 to −0.23. That bias comes from centering within short person-periods, so the jackknife is doing real work.

## 5. What Part B will decide, and what it cannot

The Part B rules are in the register addendum and in `decide_part_b.py`, both written before any data access.

- **If within T1 does not fire:**
  - within-person NA persistence is consistent with one shared persistent dimension per person;
  - item persistence of 0.25 or more is excluded, but 0.10 is not;
  - R-005's pooled excess is then attributable to between-person heterogeneity, floors included.
- **If it fires:** the reading is "item-specific persistence, *or* response anchoring, *or* between-day loading drift". The same-day variant can exclude drift only if it also fires, and it has low power.
- **The sign test can show a second shared persistent dimension.** It cannot say whether that dimension is affect or reporting.
- **Guards:**
  - a marginal-matched null keeps each real person-period's missingness mask and item distributions, and calibrates thresholds to the real floors;
  - a statistic is declared uncalibrated, and not interpreted, if the matched null fires it in more than 10%.
- **Nothing in Part B can establish a latent emotional mechanism.**

## 6. Conceptual leaps worth testing

1. **The heterogeneity term may be a construct, not only a nuisance.**
   - Pooled − within T1 measures how much people differ in the *persistent* structure of their NA items.
   - That is close to emotion differentiation (granularity): people differ in how separately they experience worry, fear and sadness ([Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721414550708)).
   - If R-005's excess is mostly heterogeneity, it may be measuring differentiation in *dynamics*, a construct with known clinical correlates.
   - **Test:** do person-level contributions to the gap track a standard same-beep differentiation index, and do they differ by trial arm or neuroticism?
2. **Dimensionality may depend on context.**
   - The Dynamic Model of Affect proposes that PA and NA couple more tightly under stress.
   - The within-person bipolar tetrad can be computed separately on beeps after unpleasant and pleasant events (0010 has event ratings).
   - That tests whether the *persistent* dimensionality of affect changes with context. This is ER's broad question asked of state structure itself.
   - It would be exploratory on 0010; a registered version would need more data.
3. **Targets should be stated in identified quantities.**
   - Following the "anchor dilemma" in PR #11: the share of NA persistence that is shared across items (about 1/(1 + T1)) is identified from reports, whatever the reporting scale.
   - A latent NA level is not.
   - Fittingness and reachability targets that refer only to identified quantities avoid the gauge problem without anchors. Whether such targets mean anything evaluatively is an open question.

## 7. Limitations

- **The bench's reach.** It covers only the worlds in it. Real data may have structure it does not, such as:
  - loading variation within a day;
  - item-specific daily rhythms (a beep-of-day sensitivity is planned);
  - drift within a period;
  - missingness that depends on single items.
- **Efficiency.** Moment-based tests are less efficient than a correctly specified likelihood model. A DSEM-class comparison was not run.
- **The ordinal correction** assumes a latent Gaussian distribution within each person-period, with thresholds estimated from about 48 beeps. It is unstable when floors are heavy. Part B switches to the linear statistic in that regime, with a calibrated threshold.
- **The matched null** assumes heterogeneity magnitudes, taken from #45. Its pooled-T1 comparison (B8) is therefore model-dependent.
- **Inference** rests on 127 person clusters and percentile bootstrap intervals. Their calibration is checked only in the bench worlds.
- **Code review.** All code is Claude's and unreviewed. The unit tests check algebra, not statistical validity.
